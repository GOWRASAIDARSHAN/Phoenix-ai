## 2. Project Overview

PHOENIX AI is a web-based inventory and pricing decision-support application designed to help retailers identify perishable products at risk of expiring unsold.

The application allows users to explore inventory conditions, examine markdown recommendations, monitor waste risk, compare financial performance, and simulate alternative pricing strategies.

The interface consists of seven interactive modules that provide different views of inventory, pricing, expiry risk, and simulation results.

The initial prototype uses synthetic inventory data and configurable simulation assumptions.

## 10. Assumptions & Methodology

### Purpose
Explains the assumptions and calculations behind the application.

### Topics documented

- Synthetic inventory generation.
- Demand multipliers.
- Price-elasticity assumptions.
- Markdown rules.
- Expiry handling.
- Inventory movement.
- Financial formulas.
- Simulation duration.
- Random seed and reproducibility.
- Model limitations.

### Financial formulas

**Revenue**

Revenue is calculated as the sum of units sold multiplied by their actual selling prices.

**COGS**

COGS is calculated as the sum of units sold multiplied by their unit costs.

**Gross Profit**

Gross Profit = Revenue − COGS

**Gross Margin**

Gross Margin (%) = (Gross Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100

Gross margin is undefined when revenue is zero.

**Waste Reduction**

Waste Reduction (%) = ((Baseline Expired Units − Smart Markdown Expired Units) ÷ Baseline Expired Units) × 100

The waste-reduction calculation must handle a zero baseline without division errors.

### User interactions

Users can open this page to understand the assumptions behind the simulation and interpret the displayed results.

## 12. Calculation and Data Integrity Rules

The application should follow these rules:

- Inventory quantities must never become negative.
- Sales cannot exceed available stock.
- Products cannot be sold after expiry.
- Expired inventory must not be counted repeatedly.
- Revenue must use actual selling prices.
- COGS must include only the cost of units sold.
- Discounts must not be subtracted twice.
- Fixed-price and smart-markdown strategies should use comparable starting conditions.
- Dashboard metrics and analytics must be consistent with simulation results.

## 15. Known Limitations

- The prototype uses synthetic data.
- Demand estimates depend on configured assumptions.
- Price elasticity may differ between products.
- Simulated outcomes do not guarantee real-world results.
- Actual retail deployment would require reliable inventory, expiry, cost, and sales data.
- Live integration with point-of-sale and inventory systems is not assumed.
